Plate I

Priam’s Birdwing

Ornithoptera priamus

Family
Papilionidae
Wingspan
150–190 mm (male)
Range
Moluccas to New Guinea and northern Australia
  • The first birdwing ever described, named by Linnaeus in 1758 after Priam, king of Troy. He gave the great swallowtails the names of Trojan heroes.
  • The male’s green is structural colour, not pigment: microscopic ridges on the scales scatter light. The engraver could only approximate it with paint.
  • Females are larger, brown and cream, and were long thought to be a different species.
  • Caterpillars feed on poisonous Dutchman’s pipe vines and carry the toxin into adulthood, so birds learn to leave the adults alone.
